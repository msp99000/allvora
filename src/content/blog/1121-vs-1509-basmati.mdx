Two varieties dominate Indian Basmati export enquiries: 1121 and 1509. They are
often quoted side by side, and buyers new to the category sometimes treat the
choice as a simple question of price. It is not. The two behave differently in
the pot, sell into different shelves, and carry different risks on a long
contract.

Here is how to decide between them.

## What separates them

Both are long-grain Basmati and both are supplied in Raw, Steam and Sella forms.
The difference that matters commercially is grain length and what the market
will pay for it.

| | 1121 | 1509 |
|---|---|---|
| Raw grain length | approximately 8.3 mm and above | approximately 8.2 mm and above |
| Grade description | Premium extra long | Long grain |
| Processing forms | Raw, Steam, Sella, Golden Sella | Raw, Steam, Sella |
| Usual position | premium retail and food service | commercial volume |

That looks like a small gap on paper. In practice, 1121 has been the reference
extra-long-grain Basmati for the export trade for years, and buyers who sell on
grain length specify it by name. 1509 is a shorter-duration crop, which
generally makes it the more competitively priced of the two.

## Cooking behaviour

Grain length is what the buyer sees before cooking. Elongation is what the
buyer sees after.

1121 is specified for strong cooking elongation, which is why it holds its
position in markets where the cooked grain is judged on the plate: biryani
houses, catering, and retail packs sold on appearance. Where elongation ratio
matters to your customer, write it into the specification rather than assuming
it.

1509 cooks well and holds a clean grain, but it is not usually the variety a
buyer reaches for when the selling point is the longest possible cooked grain.
It is the variety a buyer reaches for when they need dependable Basmati
character at volume.

## Choosing a processing form

The variety sets the grain. The processing form sets how it cooks and how it
looks on the shelf, and the two decisions are separate.

- **Raw** is milled without heat treatment. It keeps the finest aroma and the
  most slender grain, and suits markets that cook rice gently.
- **Steam** is soaked and steamed before milling. Steaming firms the grain and
  retains aroma, without the colour shift of parboiling.
- **Sella** is parboiled. The grain cooks firm and separate with a low breakage
  rate, which suits food service and high-volume kitchens.
- **Golden Sella** is parboiled to a deeper golden colour, and is specified for
  retail markets that read the golden hue as a mark of quality.

Note that Golden Sella is offered on 1121 and not on 1509 in our range. If your
market specifically wants the golden retail presentation, that narrows the
decision for you.

## The parameters to write down

Whichever variety you choose, the contract is where the money is. These are the
parameters buyers can specify on Indian Basmati:

Grain length, broken percentage, moisture, chalky grains, damaged grains,
foreign matter, admixture, purity, aroma, cooking time, elongation ratio,
ageing, crop year and sortex level.

Three of those cause most of the disputes:

**Broken percentage.** This is a contract parameter, not a fixed property of the
variety. State the maximum you accept and have it confirmed against the lot
before shipment, with the result on the Certificate of Analysis.

**Admixture.** A lot sold as 1121 can carry a percentage of other varieties.
Specify the limit. If a quote is unusually cheap for the stated variety,
admixture is the first thing to check.

**Crop year and ageing.** Aged Basmati cooks differently from fresh crop, and
some markets pay for age. If ageing matters to your customer, it belongs in the
specification with the crop year, not in a verbal assurance.

## So which should you import?

Choose **1121** if your customer buys on grain length and cooked appearance, if
you sell into premium retail or food service, or if you need Golden Sella for a
market that expects the golden pack.

Choose **1509** if you are supplying commercial volume where Basmati character
matters but the extra millimetre does not carry a price premium in your market,
or if landed cost is the constraint that decides the order.

A number of buyers run both: 1121 for the branded line, 1509 for the value line
or for food service. If that is your situation, quote them together and keep the
specification sheets separate, because the acceptance criteria are not the same.
