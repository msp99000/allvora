Indian black pepper is graded two different ways at the same time, and offers
move between the two systems without always saying so. A buyer comparing
"TGEB" against "550 GL" is comparing a screen size against a bulk density.
They are not the same measurement, and one does not convert cleanly into the
other.

This guide sets out what each grade name means, so you can read an offer sheet
and know what you are being quoted.

## The two grading systems

**Screen size** measures the diameter of the berry. Pepper is passed over
screens, and the grade name records the aperture it stays on. This is how the
Tellicherry grades are described.

**Bulk density** measures how much a given volume of pepper weighs, in grams per
litre. Denser pepper generally means fuller, heavier berries and fewer light or
hollow ones. This is how the Malabar Garbled and GL grades are described.

Both are proxies for the same underlying thing: sound, well-filled berries. They
just approach it from different directions, and each suits a different kind of
buyer.

## The Tellicherry grades

Tellicherry grades are the large-berry, appearance-led end of the range.

**TGSEB, Tellicherry Special Extra Bold.** Screened at 4.75 mm and above, with
high bulk density. This is the top size grade of Indian black pepper. Buyers
specify berry size, density, moisture at or below 11 percent, piperine and
foreign matter. It goes to spice houses, grinders and retail brands that sell
pepper on the look of the berry, in mills and in clear packaging.

**TGEB, Tellicherry Extra Bold.** Screened at 4.25 mm and above. One size step
below TGSEB, and the more widely traded of the two. The same parameters apply:
berry size, bulk density, moisture and piperine.

If your product shows the peppercorn to the customer, you are usually in this
part of the range.

## The Malabar Garbled grades

"Garbled" means cleaned: stalks, light berries and foreign matter removed.

**MG1, Malabar Garbled Grade 1.** The standard cleaned grade from the Malabar
coast, sold on bulk density rather than screen size alone. Specified on berry
size, bulk density, moisture, light berries and piperine. This is the working
grade for grinders, blenders and food manufacturers, and it carries a great deal
of the volume.

**MG2, Malabar Garbled Grade 2.** A step below MG1, specified on density,
moisture, foreign matter and piperine.

If the pepper is going to be ground, MG1 is usually the sensible starting point.
Paying a size premium for berries you are about to mill is a common and
avoidable mistake.

## The GL grades

GL is simply grams per litre. The number is the target bulk density.

- **500 GL.** Commercial grade, around 500 g/L, specified on density, moisture
  and berry size.
- **550 GL.** Premium commercial, around 550 g/L, with piperine specified.
- **600 GL.** Premium, around 600 g/L, with piperine and purity specified.

Higher density generally correlates with fuller berries, lower light-berry
content and a better yield when ground. Because GL is a direct measurement
rather than a named trade grade, it is often the clearest way to write a
contract: a number either is met or it is not.

## Piperine, and why it is on the sheet

Piperine is the compound responsible for pepper's pungency. It is reported as a
percentage and it is a standard parameter across the Indian grades.

Buyers who blend to a target heat, or who supply manufacturers working to a
fixed recipe, should specify piperine rather than infer it from the grade name.
Size and density describe the berry. Piperine describes what it does in the
food.

## How to write the specification

Whichever grade name is on the offer, the specification should state the
measurements, not just the label:

- Screen size in millimetres, or bulk density in g/L, or both
- Moisture, as a maximum percentage
- Piperine, as a minimum percentage
- Light berries and foreign matter, as maximum percentages
- Any microbiological limits your destination requires

Then confirm that a Certificate of Analysis for the shipped lot will report
against those numbers. A grade name on an invoice is a description. A COA
against stated parameters is a check.

## A short way to choose

- Selling whole peppercorns on appearance: look at **TGSEB** or **TGEB**.
- Grinding or blending: look at **MG1**, or specify a **GL** number directly.
- Working to a fixed heat level: specify **piperine** whatever else you choose.
- Comparing two offers: convert both to the same measurement before you compare
  the price, or you are not comparing anything.
